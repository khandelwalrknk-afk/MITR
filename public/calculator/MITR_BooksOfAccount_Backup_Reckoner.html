1. Specified Profession
Sec 62(1)(a) & 62(4) · Rule 46
Category A — Receipts ≤ ₹1,50,000 (or Newly Set Up with receipts not likely to exceed)
A specified professional whose gross receipts stayed at or below ₹1,50,000 in each of the three preceding Tax Years. For a freshly started practice, Category A applies when receipts for the current year are not expected to cross ₹1,50,000. No specific books are mandated by the Board for this group.
Category B — Receipts > ₹1,50,000 in Any One or More of 3 Preceding Tax Years
A specified professional whose gross receipts topped ₹1,50,000 in any one or more of the three preceding Tax Years — or, for a new practice, is expected to do so this year. This group must keep the books listed under Rule 46(4):
  • a
    Cash book — updated daily, reflecting the running cash balance at the close of each day (or at least at the end of each month)
  • b
    Journal — required only where the books follow the mercantile (accrual) system
  • c
    Ledger
  • d
    Duplicate copies of any bill or receipt the person issues for ₹250 or more
  • e
    Original supporting bills and receipts for any expense of ₹250 or more
  • f
    Self-signed payment vouchers for expenses under ₹250 where the cash book lacks adequate detail for them
Medical practitioners (any system of medicine — doctors, surgeons, dentists, pathologists, radiologists, vaids, hakims, and the like) must also keep:
  • i
    A patient-wise daily register in Form 25 (formerly Form 3C) recording the date, patient, service provided, fee charged and when it was received
  • ii
    A broad-head stock summary of drugs, medicines and consumables held — taken at both the opening and closing of the Tax Year
2. Non-Specified Profession & Business
Sec 62(1)(b) & 62(2)
Category C — Below Threshold
A non-specified profession or business that stayed within the income and turnover limits (below) across all three preceding years — and, if newly started, is not expected to breach them. No books are obligatory for this group.
Category D — Threshold Crossed or Presumptive (Lower Profit)
A non-specified profession or business that had income or turnover above the limits specified below in any one or more of the 3 preceding years — and, if newly started, is expected to breach them. Books must be kept, but the Board hasn't fixed a specific format — whatever records are needed to work out taxable income, with the Assessing Officer free to specify particulars. Two situations land here:
  • 1
    A non-specified profession or business that crossed the income or turnover limits (below).
  • 2
    A presumptive-scheme assessee under Sec 58(2)/61(2) [earlier Sec 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE, 44BB, 44BBB] who reports profit below the deemed rate.
Threshold — Individual / HUF
Income > ₹2,50,000 OR Total Sales/Turnover > ₹25,00,000
Threshold — Other Persons (Firm, Company etc.)
Income > ₹1,20,000 OR Total Sales/Turnover > ₹10,00,000
Retention & Location
1
How long (Rule 46(9)): retain the books and records for 7 tax years measured from the close of the relevant Tax Year.
2
Reassessment (Rule 46(10)): if a year is reopened under Sec 279 (or Sec 147 of the 1961 Act as it stood before repeal), whatever records existed when it was reopened stay preserved until that reassessment is completed.
3
Where: Keep them at the main place of practice. Running multiple locations? Either hold everything centrally, or keep each location's separate set on-site there.
Other Key Points
→
After a business closes (Sec 320(4)): money collected once a business or profession has shut down is taxed in the hands of whoever receives it, in the year it comes in.
→
Electronic records (Rule 46(8)): books kept in electronic form must stay accessible within India at all times, and a daily back-up has to be held on servers physically located in India.
→
First year of practice: for a newly started activity, the category is decided on what the year's receipts, income or turnover are expected to reach, rather than past figures.

Film Artist — Definition
A film artist is anyone who works professionally on making a cinematograph film (whether they produce it themselves or work for someone else). It covers:
  • Actor
  • Cameraman
  • Director (including assistant director)
  • Music director (including assistant music director)
  • Art director (including assistant art director)
  • Dance director (including assistant dance director)
  • Editor
  • Singer
  • Lyricist
  • Story writer
  • Screenplay writer
  • Dialogue writer
  • Dress designer
Authorised Representative — Definition
An authorised representative is someone who, for a fee or remuneration, represents another person before:
  • Any Tribunal; or
  • Any authority set up under any law in force
It does not include:
  • An employee of the person being represented
  • Someone in the legal profession
  • Someone in the accountancy profession
Non-Specified Profession & Business — Sec 62(1)(b)
This covers any business, and any profession that is not in the specified list above. Whether books are needed depends on income/turnover:
  • Below the thresholds → no books required (Category C)
  • Thresholds crossed → books required, but no specific format is prescribed — keep whatever records let you compute income; the AO may ask for particulars (Category D)
  • Presumptive with lower profit → if declaring profit below the deemed rate under Sec 58(2)/61(2), books become mandatory (Category D)
Thresholds: Individual/HUF — income above ₹2,50,000 or turnover above ₹25 lakh. Others — income above ₹1,20,000 or turnover above ₹10 lakh. (See Prescribed Books tab.)

→

Companies Act, 2013 — Section 128

Books of account, etc., to be kept by company

Why this matters
A company maintaining books for income-tax must independently satisfy Sec 128 of the Companies Act. The two regimes run in parallel — note the longer 8-year retention here versus 7 years under Rule 46(9).
128(1) — What & Where
Every company must prepare and keep, at its registered office, books of account, other relevant books and papers, and financial statements for each financial year, giving a true and fair view of its affairs (including any branch). Books must be kept on the accrual basis and under the double-entry system.
  • i
    Books may be kept at another place in India if the Board so decides — the company must then file a written notice of that address in Form AOC-5 with the Registrar within 7 days
  • ii
    Books may also be kept in electronic mode in the prescribed manner
128(2) — Branch Offices
A company with a branch (in India or abroad) is treated as compliant if proper books for the branch's transactions are kept at that branch and periodic summarised returns are sent to the registered office (or the other notified place).
128(3) — Inspection
Books kept in India are open for inspection at the registered office (or other notified place) by any director during business hours. Financial information held outside India must be made available in copy for inspection, subject to prescribed conditions.
Inspection of a subsidiary's books may be done only by a person authorised by a Board resolution.
128(4) — Assistance During Inspection
Officers and employees must give the person carrying out the inspection all assistance that the company may reasonably be expected to provide.
128(5) — Retention Period
Books of account, together with relevant vouchers, must be kept in good order for at least 8 financial years immediately preceding the current financial year (or for all preceding years, if the company is younger than 8 years).
If an investigation is ordered under Chapter XIV, the Central Government may direct a longer retention period.
128(6) — Penalty for Contravention
If the Managing Director, the Whole-time Director in charge of finance, the CFO, or any other person charged by the Board with compliance contravenes this section, that person is liable to a fine of not less than ₹50,000, extending up to ₹5,00,000.
The earlier provision for imprisonment up to one year has been omitted.
Electronic Records — Annual Intimation to Registrar (Rule 3(6))
Where books are maintained in electronic mode, the company must intimate the Registrar each year, at the time of filing the financial statements:
  • a
    Name of the service provider
  • b
    Internet Protocol (IP) address of the service provider
  • c
    Location of the service provider (wherever applicable)
  • d
    Where books are maintained on the cloud — the address provided by the service provider
  • e
    Where the service provider is located outside India — the name and address of the person in control of the books in India

GST — Accounts & Records

Books of account required under Section 35 of the CGST Act & Rule 56 of the CGST Rules

Statutory Basis
Section 35 of the CGST Act sets out the core accounts every registered person must keep; Rule 56 of the CGST Rules prescribes the detailed records, special-category accounts and integrity rules. Books may be physical or electronic, kept at the declared place(s) of business.
Section 35 — Core Accounts
What every registered person keeps
At the principal place of business, a true and correct account of:
  • a
    Production or manufacture of goods
  • b
    Inward and outward supply of goods or services (or both)
  • c
    Stock of goods
  • d
    Input tax credit availed
  • e
    Output tax payable and paid
  • f
    Such other particulars as may be prescribed
Where more than one place of business is on the registration certificate, the accounts for each place are kept at that place. Records may be maintained electronically. Warehouse/godown operators and transporters must keep records of consignor, consignee and goods — whether registered or not.
Rule 56 — Detailed Records
Registers & documents (all registered persons)
  • 1
    Imports, exports and reverse-charge supplies, with invoices, bills of supply, delivery challans, credit/debit notes and receipt/payment/refund vouchers
  • 2
    Stock account — opening balance, receipts, supplies, goods lost/stolen/destroyed/written off/gifted/free-sampled, and closing balance (raw materials, finished goods, scrap, wastage). Not required for composition taxpayers under Sec 10.
  • 3
    Separate account of advances received, paid and adjusted
  • 4
    Tax account — tax payable (incl. reverse charge), collected and paid, input tax, ITC claimed, plus a register of invoices, credit/debit notes and delivery challans. Not required for composition taxpayers.
  • 5
    Names and addresses of suppliers and recipients, and addresses of premises where goods are stored (including in transit)
Special-category accounts
  • →
    Agents — authorisation from each principal, goods/services received and supplied per principal, accounts furnished and tax paid
  • →
    Manufacturers — monthly production accounts with quantitative details of raw materials/services used and goods manufactured (incl. waste and by-products)
  • →
    Service providers — quantitative details of goods used, input services utilised and services supplied
  • →
    Works contractors — separate accounts per contract: parties, goods/services received and used, payments received, and suppliers
  • →
    Carriers / C&F agents — true records of goods handled on behalf of any registered person
Integrity & Location Rules
How records must be kept
  • →
    No erasing or overwriting: incorrect entries (other than clerical) are scored out under attestation and the correct entry recorded. For electronic records, a log of every edit/deletion must be kept.
  • →
    Manual books: each volume serially numbered.
  • →
    Electronic records: permitted, authenticated by digital signature.
  • →
    Location: at the principal place of business and at each additional place on the registration certificate; digital records must be accessible at every related place.
  • →
    Goods found stored at an undeclared place without valid documents — the proper officer may tax them as if supplied by the registered person.
Retention Period (Sec 36)
Standard Period
72 months (6 years) from the due date of furnishing the annual return for that financial year
Disputes / Litigation
Where the registered person is party to an appeal, revision or investigation, records must be kept for one year after final disposal of the proceeding, or until the standard 72-month period ends — whichever is later.
Penalty for Non-Compliance
Failure to maintain proper accounts can attract a general penalty of up to ₹25,000 under the CGST/SGST Act. Separately, where goods or services are not accounted for, the proper officer may determine and recover the tax as if those goods/services had been supplied (Sec 35(6)).

Company Compliance — Books & Backup

A single, unified checklist for a company subject to Income-tax, Companies Act and GST — strictest period applied throughout

How to read this
One unified set of obligations for an operating company. Where the underlying laws differ on a period or deadline, the strictest requirement is applied throughout — so following this keeps the company compliant on every front without tracking each law separately.
1. Books & Records to Maintain
Maintain one set of accounts on the accrual basis using the double-entry system, giving a true and fair view. Keep:
  • →
    Cash book, bank book, journal and ledger
  • →
    Sales/outward and purchase/inward supply registers (with invoice and tax details)
  • →
    Stock register — incl. scrap, wastage, and goods lost/stolen/destroyed/written off/gifted/free-sampled
  • →
    ITC register; output tax register; account of advances
  • →
    Fixed asset register and depreciation schedule
  • →
    Tax invoices, bills of supply, credit/debit notes, delivery challans, vouchers; supplier/recipient/storage details
  • →
    Financial statements (P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow — Schedule III)
  • →
    Manufacturers: monthly production accounts · Service providers: input-service details
2. How Records Must Be Kept
  • →
    Physical or electronic. No erasing/overwriting — score out incorrect entries (other than clerical) under attestation; keep an edit/deletion log for electronic records.
  • →
    Manual volumes serially numbered; electronic records in original/legible format, complete and unaltered.
  • →
    Accounting software must carry a non-disableable audit trail (mandatory FY 2023-24 onwards).
  • →
    Electronic records authenticated (e.g. digital signature) and accessible in India at all times.
3. Electronic Backup — Mandatory Daily
New & immediate
The daily-backup-in-India requirement is new for income-tax, effective 1 April 2026 (records from TY 2026-27). Begin immediately — failure is reported by the tax auditor and may attract penalties.
  • →
    Daily back-up stored on servers physically located in India — including a copy of any data held overseas. Anchored in Rule 46(8), IT Rules 2026 and mirrored by the MCA's Companies (Accounts) Rule 3(5).
  • →
    Document an explicit day-end cut-off time (e.g. 23:59 IST) in policy and produce the daily run logs with a SHA-256 hash per file to evidence integrity.
  • →
    Tiered retention: daily incremental + weekly full (1–4 weeks) · monthly full (6–12 months) · one year-end full (8 years). Daily India-resident snapshots kept for 7 tax years per Rule 46(9).
  • →
    3-2-1 rule (3 copies, 2 media, 1 offsite); immutable/WORM long-term archive; quarterly restore tests.
4. Retention Period
Strictest standard
Retain all books, records, vouchers and backups for at least 8 financial years from the end of the financial year
Keep them longer where any proceeding is pending — assessment, reassessment, appeal, revision or investigation — until that matter is finally concluded, even if 8 years have lapsed.
5. Where to Keep the Books
  • →
    Primary books at the registered office (usually also the principal place of business).
  • →
    To keep them elsewhere in India — Board resolution + Form AOC-5 to the Registrar within 7 days.
  • →
    Multi-location: keep location-specific records at each site; electronic records accessible from every related place.
6. Consequences of Default
  • →
    Income Tax: failure to maintain books — ₹25,000 penalty on the company. Incorrect information in an audit report/certificate — ₹10,000 on the accountant (CA) for each such report/certificate (Sec 271J), no intent required, relief only on proof of reasonable cause. Backup details not reported, or reported incorrectly, fall in this category.
  • →
    GST: general penalty up to ₹25,000 (CGST/SGST); unaccounted goods/services may be taxed as if supplied.
  • →
    Company-law default: fine ₹50,000 to ₹5,00,000 on the responsible officer (MD / WTD-finance / CFO / charged person).

Electronic Backup — Practical Guidance

Best-practice strategy for meeting the daily back-up requirement (Rule 46(8) / Rule 3) without overwhelming storage

New Requirement — Act Now
The daily electronic back-up rule is a new requirement under the Income Tax Act 2025, applicable from Tax Year 2026-27 onwards. Compliance should begin immediately. Non-compliance will be reported by the tax auditor in Form 26 and may attract penalties.
Rule 46(8), IT Rules 2026 — Legal Baseline
Where books of account are maintained electronically, Rule 46(8) (effective 1 April 2026) requires: (1) the data must remain accessible in India at all times; (2) a daily back-up — not periodic; and (3) stored on servers physically located in India, ruling out overseas-only cloud storage of primary tax records. This applies to all individuals, HUFs and professionals required to keep books under Section 62, for records from Tax Year 2026-27 onwards. The guidance below is practical IT best practice for achieving this — it is not itself a statutory requirement.
Who is covered — Applicability under Sec 62 & Sec 63
Rule 46(8) reaches every person required to keep books under the Act whose records are maintained electronically. Two thresholds drive coverage:
  • 62
    Sec 62 (Maintenance): Individuals / HUFs in business — income > ₹2.5 L or turnover > ₹25 L in any of the 3 preceding years; specified professionals over the statutory limits; Companies, LLPs and partnership firms — automatic, no turnover threshold.
  • 63
    Sec 63 (Tax Audit): Businesses > ₹1 Cr turnover — scaling to ₹10 Cr where ≥95% of receipts/payments flow through banking or prescribed electronic modes; professionals > ₹50 L gross receipts.
Mirrors the MCA Companies (Accounts) Rules daily-backup standard — Rule 46(8) extends it to all categories of digital-records taxpayers, not just companies.
Penalty & Collateral Risk — Why ₹25,000 Isn't the Real Number
FailureDirect fineCollateral statutory impact
Daily backup not maintained on Indian servers ₹25,000 Rejection of electronic records → Best-Judgment Assessment by AO (estimated profit, disallowed expenses, demand notice).
Incorrect certification by tax auditor ₹10,000 Audit qualification; high-risk flag in CBDT insight engines; recurring scrutiny exposure.
The teeth: if the AO holds that the daily-backup lifecycle was broken or kept on unverified offshore servers, the books can be rejected wholesale — opening the door to Best-Judgment Assessment where the AO estimates net profit on industry averages, disallows legitimate expenses, and raises a demand on the inflated figure. The ₹25,000 fine is dwarfed by the BJA exposure.
Interpreting "Daily" — The Day-End Cut-off
Random, weekly or ad-hoc snapshots do not satisfy the rule. The phrase "daily at the close of every business day" is read strictly by tax authorities.
  • →
    Single-shift business: backup at the end of local operational hours.
  • →
    24/7 or multi-timezone operations (e-commerce, IT services, SaaS): fix an explicit cut-off (e.g. 23:59 IST), document it in the internal compliance policy, and run the automated backup at that exact interval every day.
  • →
    Auditors look for evidence of the documented cut-off — keep the policy on letterhead and produce it alongside the daily run logs.
The Cloud Trap — Access ≠ Data Residency
Logging into a foreign-hosted ERP from an office terminal in Mumbai or Pune does not satisfy the data-localisation requirement. The physical backup must sit on a server inside India.
  • !
    If the primary ERP/SaaS runs on Singapore, Ireland, US-East or any other overseas region, set up an independent secondary pipeline that replicates accounting data to an India-resident node daily.
  • !
    Secure written confirmation from the vendor that both the primary and backup data centres are physically located in India — retain it for the auditor.
1. Recommended Backup Types
Daily — Incremental
Backs up only the data changed since the previous day. Fast to run and storage-efficient, which makes a genuine daily back-up practical.
Weekly / Monthly — Full
A complete back-up each weekend or month creates a clean baseline and shortens the incremental chain, which speeds up recovery when you actually need to restore.
2. Tiered Retention (Grandfather–Father–Son)
Keeping daily increments for 7–8 years is impractical and the storage cost balloons. Instead, cascade the retention:
Tier What to keep How long
Short-term Daily incremental + weekly full 1 to 4 weeks
Medium-term Monthly full backups 6 to 12 months
Long-term (compliance) One year-end full backup 7 to 8 years
The single year-end archive satisfies legal, tax and audit retention without storing every daily increment for years.
3. Compliance & Storage Best Practices
The 3-2-1 Rule
Keep 3 copies of data, on 2 different media types (e.g. cloud + local server), with 1 copy offsite.
Indian Legal Requirement
For electronic books of account under the Income-tax Rules, a daily back-up must be taken and stored on servers physically located in India.
Immutable Backups
Protect the long-term 7–8 year archives with WORM (Write Once, Read Many) storage or ransomware protection so the data cannot be tampered with.
4. Automate It — Don't Rely on Memory
Why this matters
A daily back-up is now a legal obligation, but a manual daily task is the easiest thing to forget — and one missed day is a compliance gap the auditor must report. The only reliable way to meet a daily requirement is to automate it so it runs without anyone remembering.
Schedule it
Set the backup to run automatically every day at a fixed time (e.g. after close of business) via the accounting software's built-in scheduler, the operating system's task scheduler, or your cloud provider's backup policy. No human step in the daily loop.
Alert on failure
Configure email/SMS alerts so that if a backup fails or is skipped, the responsible person is notified the same day and can fix it — rather than discovering the gap months later at audit.
Log every run
Keep an automatic log of each backup (date, time, success/failure, destination server). These run records are themselves part of compliance and are exactly what the auditor checks.
Verify automatically
Use a managed/cloud service that confirms each backup landed on an India-located server, and schedule a periodic automated restore test so a silent corruption doesn't go unnoticed.
Cryptographic hash on every backup
Pair each daily backup file with a SHA-256 (or MD5) hash and a timestamped log capturing execution time, file size, destination IP/location, and the hash. The hash chain is what proves the records weren't edited after the fact — and is exactly what the auditor inspects.
Retention pipeline — Rule 46(9)
Automate archival rules to preserve the daily India-resident snapshots for the statutory 7 tax years under Rule 46(9). Pair this with a year-end full backup retained for the longer 8-year window so both Income-tax and Companies Act bases are covered.
Note
Backup design depends on data volume, industry and whether you run cloud, local or hybrid infrastructure. Treat this as a starting framework and tailor it to your environment.

1. What this policy is for

Because a daily backup is easy to forget, we automate it rather than rely on anyone to remember:
  • →
    The daily backup runs automatically at a set time each day, with no manual step.
  • →

  • →
    Every run is logged (date, time, success/failure, destination) as a compliance record.

  • →
    A backup is taken every day and stored on a server located in India (required from Tax Year 2026-27).

    The accounting software keeps an audit trail (a tamper-proof log of every entry and edit) that cannot be switched off.
  • →
    Once a year we inform the Registrar of the software/service provider details (name, location, and cloud address if used).

  • →
